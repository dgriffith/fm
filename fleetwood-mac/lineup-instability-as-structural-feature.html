Lineup Instability as Structural Feature

Fleetwood Mac holds an unusual distinction: they are probably the only major rock band whose pre-fame lineup is considered by a significant portion of their fanbase to be superior to the famous one, and they achieved this while also cycling through enough members to populate a small village. Since 1967, over 20 musicians have been official members. The question of whether this instability is a bug or a structural feature of the band’s identity is worth taking seriously.

The McVie-Fleetwood Center as Anchor

The organizational logic that makes the name “Fleetwood Mac” coherent across such radical personnel change is simple: Mick Fleetwood and John McVie have been in virtually every iteration, and they collectively own the name (along with its commercial and legal apparatus). This means that when a lineup dissolves, the question is not “is this still Fleetwood Mac” but “what will Fleetwood Mac do next.” The name functions less like a band identifier and more like a franchise mark. Fleetwood has been explicit about this in interviews — the name is the vehicle; the current members are whoever can best drive it.

Typology of Departures

The departures fall into a few categories. Mental health crisis: Peter Green, Jeremy Spencer (cult membership is a variant), Danny Kirwan. Voluntary withdrawal citing personal reasons: Christine McVie (1998–2014). Creative conflict leading to firing or coerced exit: Lindsey Buckingham (1987 departure framed as self-directed; 2018 firing more clearly forced). Departure for other opportunities: Bob Welch. Each category produces a different kind of aftermath and a different mythology.

The 1987–1995 Lineup Question

The most contested lineup question concerns the 1987–1993 period following Buckingham’s departure. Mick Fleetwood recruited Billy Burnette, Rick Vito, and eventually Bekka Bramlett and Dave Mason, producing Behind the Mask (1990) and Time (1995). These albums sold poorly and are critically dismissed, but they represent a genuine attempt to continue the band rather than simply coast on legacy. Whether they “count” as Fleetwood Mac in any meaningful sense depends on whether you think the band’s identity is organizational (in which case yes) or attached to specific creative voices (in which case, debatable).
