Buckingham’s Firing in 2018 and the Final Chapter Debate

The firing of Lindsey Buckingham in April 2018 — mid-preparation for a world tour behind the Lindsey Buckingham/Christine McVie album — is the most recent major rupture in the band’s history and the event that makes serious discussion of the band’s “final chapter” newly relevant. The circumstances were sufficiently acrimonious to produce a lawsuit, and the competing accounts are informative about the internal politics of the late-period band.

What Happened

The public account, pieced together from multiple sources: a pre-Grammy rehearsal in early 2018 went badly, involving a confrontation between Buckingham and Nicks that escalated to Nicks issuing an ultimatum to Mick Fleetwood — either Buckingham goes or she does. Fleetwood, whose organizational and financial interests were aligned with the touring entity, chose to let Buckingham go. Buckingham claimed he was fired; the band’s initial public statement said he and the band had “mutually agreed to part ways.” Buckingham filed suit against the remaining members for lost touring income; the case was settled out of court in 2018 for an undisclosed sum.

The Nicks Ultimatum

The specifics of what triggered the confrontation remain contested. Buckingham’s account suggests a dispute over tour scheduling (he wanted a shorter tour to accommodate a solo album cycle; the band wanted a longer commitment). Nicks’s side suggests a more personal grievance related to comments Buckingham made at the Grammys ceremony. Both accounts are probably partially accurate; the proximate cause was likely the Grammys moment, but the underlying tension — over creative control, over Nicks’s authority relative to Buckingham’s, over the band’s identity as legacy act versus ongoing creative project — had been accumulating for decades.

The Replacement Lineup

Neil Finn (of Crowded House) and Mike Campbell (Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers) replaced Buckingham for the 2018–2019 tour. Both are accomplished musicians with strong catalogues of their own, and by most accounts the tour was a professional success. Whether it was Fleetwood Mac in any meaningful creative sense is the question the band’s final-chapter debate revolves around. Christine McVie’s death in November 2022 has since made the question of a classic-lineup restoration moot.
