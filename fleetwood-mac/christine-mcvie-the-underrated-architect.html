Christine McVie — The Underrated Architect

Christine McVie is the most underrated member of Fleetwood Mac’s classic lineup in critical discourse, which is a striking fact given that she wrote some of the band’s most beloved songs and served as a consistent organizational and emotional center during the most turbulent periods of the band’s history. The underrating seems to have multiple causes: her quieter public persona relative to Nicks’s theatrical self-presentation; the relative sobriety of her personal life compared to the soap opera around her; and the critical tendency to privilege confessional drama over craft.

Compositional Signature

McVie’s songs are structurally economical in a way that can masquerade as simplicity. “Say You Love Me,” “You Make Loving Fun,” “Oh Well,” “Little Lies,” “Everywhere,” “Hold Me” — these are all built on short melodic cells that expand through arrangement rather than harmonic complexity. Her chord progressions are often deceptively simple, but the voicings and the relationship between melody and bass line are carefully thought out. The adult-pop accessibility of these songs is an achievement, not an accident.

Her piano playing — she trained classically before joining the blues circuit — gives the band’s recorded sound a harmonic richness that Buckingham’s guitar-forward production might otherwise have lacked. The interplay between her left-hand bass lines and John McVie’s bass on tracks like “The Chain” (where their parts lock in a way that feels compositionally inevitable) is one of the textural signatures of the classic period.

Temperamental Role

Multiple accounts from the Rumours and Tusk sessions describe McVie as a de-escalating presence — the person most likely to interrupt a conflict between Buckingham and Nicks with a practical question about the arrangement. This temperamental role has perhaps made her seem less dramatically interesting to writers reconstructing the period, but from a production standpoint, the ability to keep a session functional while three other people are dissolving relationships is its own kind of creative contribution.

Departure, Return, and Late Work

McVie’s 1998 departure was attributed to aerophobia — she refused to fly, which made a touring band an impractical career. Her 2014 return, following reportedly successful treatment, led to the Lindsey Buckingham/Christine McVie album (2017) and a reunion tour, before Buckingham’s firing changed the configuration again. Her death in November 2022 closed the book on any further classic-lineup activity. See Fleetwood Mac — Origins and the Peter Green Era for her first appearance in the band’s history.
